# Journeys, step by step

What was designed and agreed on 24 September 2026, in order. Each step has its screens in the matching Screens group.

## 1 · Where the user lives

Three structures were compared: the conversation at the center (A), the product at the center (B) and a focus desk (C). **B was chosen: the product blueprint.** The product is the center; the conversation is a tool attached to whatever is selected ("Ask DEMIURGO about this").

## 2 · Views of the product

Tabs under Product:
- **Overview:** purpose, who uses it, rules, features, and Needs you.
- **Map:** areas with *needs*, *affects* and *conflicts* relations.
- **Origins:** where everything comes from, as a trace read left to right.
- **Journeys:** how people will use the product, with the undefined gaps shown inside the flow.

The person asked for trees, flows and canvases that help understand everything.

## 3 · One language (closed)

- Dots say how sure, bars say how far, color says what it asks of you, and icons say what it is.
- "Decided" became **Confirmed**. Confirmed and Assumed have no color of their own.
- The stage became the three-bar track after the person rejected a "Ready to build" pill: "very ugly, too much text, badge too big".
- Replaced got its own mark: two overlapping circles.
- There is one card template in three sizes.
- Point, peek, keep was called "a perfect system".

## 4 · Day 1

A clickable path from a blank page to a first map:
1. one open question, "What do you want to build?";
2. DEMIURGO reads the idea live;
3. "Here's what I understood", everything Proposed and quoting the person's words, confirmed in one step;
4. at most three questions, one at a time, each with a recommendation and what it affects;
5. a summary that ends with "You can close DEMIURGO".

The legend of marks was the person's idea. The step was agreed with "OK to everything".

## 5 · Review a feature

1. The feature at a glance: what it is for, how it works, its rules, its checks with who verifies them, and the readiness in product words.
2. A guided review in five parts on the same page. The first part is **Context**, added at the person's request: where it comes from, what it changes and what it touches.
3. A change redrawn in blue, with its impact and a warning when something can't be verified.
4. Ready to build.

Agreed with "Perfect, really".

## 6 · Coming back

1. The person lands on the product with a "what changed" lens, and "While you were away" tells what happened in a few lines.
2. Catching up goes one thing at a time, in the order that unblocks most: first a conflict, then a question, then a feature to review. It can be left at any moment.
3. "You're up to date" closes the step.

Also agreed: a rust bar means the stage is in doubt, and DEMIURGO never picks between two confirmed things that clash. Agreed with "Perfect".

## 7 · An agent proposes (reframed)

The person corrected the frame. DEMIURGO is agnostic to the agent: it runs its own models through providers, and the person chooses the model and the reasoning for each kind of task. Nothing is ever pasted into a terminal. The channel from the terminal is only for testing.

- Screens 1 and 3 are kept but not for now.
- Screen 2 (proposals one at a time, out of date, reject with a reason) is the pattern for any agent batch in Needs you.
- *Models & providers* replaces the Agents tab.

## 8 · Back to H1

The design was realigned to the next milestone, and nothing was thrown away. H1 is done when the FDR of S3 is born and approved inside DEMIURGO, with the person in the UI. The page maps:
- the seven stops of that path;
- what each earlier screen gives H1;
- the marks, checked against what the back end really stores.
